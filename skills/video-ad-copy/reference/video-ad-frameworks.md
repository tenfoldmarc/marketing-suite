# Video Ad Frameworks

Reference for the /video-ad-copy skill. Every framework below is written for Meta, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts placements: vertical, sound-on-optional, 15 to 90 seconds.

---

## 1. The retention math (why structure matters)

| Window | What the platform measures | What the script has to do |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 seconds | Hook rate (3-second views ÷ impressions). Good: 30%+. Great: 40%+. | Stop the scroll. Spoken line + on-screen text + a visual that breaks pattern. All three at once. |
| 3 to 15 seconds | Hold rate (ThruPlay or 15-second views ÷ 3-second views). Good: 25%+. | Confirm the hook was worth it. Open a curiosity loop or name the problem precisely. |
| 15 seconds to end | Completion rate. | New idea or new visual every 3 to 5 seconds. No line that doesn't move the story. |
| Last 5 seconds | Click-through. | One CTA. Say what to tap and what happens next. |

Rules that fall out of this:
- The first spoken words must be the hook. No "Hey guys," no intro, no name.
- On-screen text in the first frame carries the hook for sound-off viewers (most of them).
- Every 3 to 5 seconds something changes: camera angle, b-roll, text pop, zoom, prop.
- Say the CTA once at the end and once mid-video for 60-second-plus cuts.

## 2. Hook types (spoken openers)

Write 5 to 10 per script. Mix types. Each is one sentence, under 12 words spoken.

| Type | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Callout | "[Audience], [this is for you / stop scrolling]." | "Roofing owners doing over a million a year, this is for you." |
| Why problem | "Why do [audience] struggle to [outcome]?" | "Why do most coaches struggle to book sales calls?" |
| Contrarian | "Stop [common advice]." / "[Common belief] is wrong." | "Stop stretching your back. It's making the pain worse." |
| Mistake | "The biggest mistake [audience] make with [topic]..." | "The biggest mistake new agency owners make with cold email..." |
| Result first | "[Specific result] in [timeframe]. Here's how." | "27 booked calls in 9 days. Here's exactly what we ran." |
| Secret / real reason | "The real reason [problem] isn't [what they think]." | "The real reason your ads don't convert isn't the targeting." |
| Story drop | Start mid-scene. | "So my son's doctor wanted to put him on a fourth medication." |
| Question to self | "[Internal dialogue in quotes]" | "'What's wrong with me? Why can't I lose this weight?'" |
| Proof flash | Show the number, say the number. | "This is a $10k month from one Amazon store. She's a mom of three." |
| Warning | "If you [do X], watch this before [Y]." | "If you're about to hire an agency, watch this first." |
| Curiosity object | Hold or point at a thing. | "This piece of paper made me more money than my last 3 launches." |

## 3. Script frameworks

Pick by traffic temperature and offer type. Each block lists beats in order with rough timing for a 30 to 60 second cut.

### A. Hook, Body, CTA (the base)
1. Hook (0 to 3s)
2. Body: one idea, one proof, one objection handled (3 to 25s)
3. CTA (last 5s)
Use for: anything short, retargeting, warm traffic.

### B. Callout, Value, CTA (Hormozi)
1. Callout: name the audience and the situation (0 to 3s)
2. Value: teach or show the thing, fully, no holding back (3 to 45s)
3. CTA: "If you want the full version / the template / the call, tap below." (last 5s)
Use for: lead magnets, free trainings, cold traffic where trust is low.

### C. PAS: Problem, Agitate, Solve
1. Problem stated in their words (0 to 5s)
2. Agitate: what it costs them, what they've tried, why those failed (5 to 20s)
3. Solve: the mechanism, named, one line on why it works (20 to 35s)
4. Proof + CTA (35 to 45s)
Use for: pain-driven offers, health, money, relationships.

### D. Before, After, Bridge
1. Before: the old life, specific scene (0 to 8s)
2. After: the new life, specific scene (8 to 15s)
3. Bridge: the method that got them there (15 to 35s)
4. CTA
Use for: transformation offers, coaching, fitness, business results.

### E. UGC story (the top performer on Meta in 2026)
1. Hook: the problem or the result, said casually (0 to 3s)
2. "I tried everything" list, fast (3 to 10s)
3. Discovery moment: "then I found / a friend told me" (10 to 15s)
4. What it is and what happened, with one specific number (15 to 35s)
5. Soft CTA: "link's below if you want to try it" (last 5s)
Shot like a phone video. Natural light. Slightly imperfect. Captions on.
Use for: products, courses, apps, anything under $500.

### F. Founder to camera
1. Hook: a belief or a result (0 to 3s)
2. Origin: why they built this, the moment it clicked (3 to 20s)
3. Mechanism: what's different, named (20 to 35s)
4. Proof: clients, numbers, time in business (35 to 45s)
5. Who it's for and who it's not for (45 to 55s)
6. CTA
Use for: high ticket, services, book-a-call offers.

### G. Mistake or "Stop doing X"
1. "Stop [thing]" or "The biggest mistake..." (0 to 3s)
2. Why it's a mistake, the mechanism of the problem (3 to 15s)
3. What to do instead, 3 quick steps or one key shift (15 to 35s)
4. CTA to the full method
Use for: cold traffic, education, authority building.

### H. 3 reasons / listicle
1. "3 reasons [outcome] isn't happening" (0 to 3s)
2. Reason 1, 2, 3, each with a one-line fix, numbered on screen (3 to 40s)
3. "Number 3 is the one most people miss. Full breakdown below." CTA
Use for: warm traffic, retention, easy to shoot.

### I. Demo / screen share
1. Hook: the result on screen (0 to 3s)
2. "Watch this." Do the thing live, narrated, sped up where slow (3 to 40s)
3. Result shown again, CTA
Use for: software, AI tools, systems, anything you can show.

### J. Mini VSL (60 to 90s)
1. Hook (0 to 3s)
2. Problem and failed alternatives (3 to 15s)
3. Root cause (15 to 25s)
4. Mechanism, named (25 to 40s)
5. Proof (40 to 55s)
6. Offer in one line + what happens when they click (55 to 70s)
7. Remove the retreat: what staying the same costs (70 to 80s)
8. CTA
Use for: cold traffic to a webinar, VSL page, or application.

### K. Skit / pattern interrupt
1. A scripted scene that looks like content, not an ad (0 to 8s)
2. The turn: the scene reveals the problem (8 to 15s)
3. Straight-to-camera pivot: "Here's the fix" (15 to 35s)
4. CTA
Use for: brands with personality, DTC, younger audiences.

## 4. Body copy ingredients (same list as static ads, applied to spoken lines)

Hit at least five in any script over 30 seconds:
- Root cause / underlying problem
- Mechanism of the problem or of the solution, named
- Outcome / transformation, specific
- Proof: number, name, timeframe
- Disqualify alternatives (what they tried and why it failed)
- Absolve past failure ("it's not that you failed, the plan was wrong")
- Story beat
- Create hope
- Remove the retreat (cost of doing nothing)
- One objection handled out loud

---

## 5. On-screen text rules

- First frame: the hook in 3 to 7 words, big, high contrast, upper-middle third.
- Captions: every spoken word, karaoke or line-by-line, bottom third, inside the safe zone (top 250px and bottom 340px clear on 9:16).
- Key numbers pop as their own text card the moment they're said.
- CTA card in the last 3 seconds: "Tap Learn More" or "Link below" plus the outcome.
- Max 2 fonts. One heavy sans for hooks and numbers, one clean sans for captions.

---

## 6. Length versions

Every script ships in three cuts:

| Cut | Use | What survives |
|---|---|---|
| 15s | Stories, Reels retargeting, top-of-funnel awareness | Hook, one proof, CTA |
| 30s | Default feed ad | Hook, problem, mechanism, one proof, CTA |
| 60s+ | Cold traffic to a page with a pitch, mini VSL | Everything |

---

## 7. Delivery notes for talking head and AI avatar

- Write for the mouth, not the eye. Read it out loud. If you run out of breath, cut the sentence.
- Contractions always. "I'm," "you're," "it's."
- One idea per sentence. Period. Next sentence.
- Mark emphasis words in CAPS sparingly (1 per 2 sentences max).
- Add [pause] where a beat lands. Add [look at camera] or [hold up prop] as stage directions in brackets.
- For AI avatars (Arcads, HeyGen, Higgsfield): keep lines under 20 words each. Avoid tongue twisters and brand names the voice may mispronounce; spell phonetically in brackets if needed.
- For talking head: mark where b-roll covers the cut so the speaker can flub and restart.

---

## 8. Compliance in video

Same policy rules as static ads (see the ad-copy skill's meta-ad-specs): no personal attributes spoken at the viewer ("are you overweight?"), no guaranteed income or health outcomes, case studies need context, no engagement bait, no fake urgency. "Results vary" as a caption on any result claim.
