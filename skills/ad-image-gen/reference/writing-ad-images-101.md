# Writing Ad Images 101

Reference doc for generating high-converting Meta (Facebook / Instagram) static ad images with AI image models (OpenAI gpt-image-2 and Google Nano Banana Pro / Nano Banana 2). Built September 2026 from live Meta Ad Library swipes plus 2026 creative benchmark reports. Companion to `writing-ad-copy-101.md` (the words around the image). This doc covers the picture itself.

Swipe images live in `swipes/`. Every one is described in words in Section 8, so this file works even if the images never load.

---

## 1. What makes a static ad image convert

A static image has one job: stop the thumb and hand the reader one idea in under a second. Everything below serves that.

**The numbers that matter (2026):**
- Static images still carry most direct-response spend. Motion's 2026 Creative Benchmarks ($1.29B in Meta spend, 578,750 creatives) found "Text only" and "Product image with text" are the top two asset types by hit rate AND by spend-use ratio. Plain text on a colored box beats high-production work at finding winners.
- Only about 5% of creatives become winners (Motion). Small accounts see 3.8%, large ones 8.2% (Taylor Sicard). Plan on volume, not one perfect ad.
- Statics fatigue faster than video: 7 to 14 days at scale spend vs 14 to 28 for video (Superscale). Frequency above 2.5 to 3.0 is the early warning.
- Statics with more than about 20% of the canvas covered by text underperformed in Superscale's testing. Big text, not lots of text.

**The nine rules:**

1. **Stop the scroll with contrast, not decoration.** In a static, "motion" is contrast: a bright subject on a dark ground, red on white, a face against a flat color. The contrast has to mean something (a transformation, a product in use, an emotion), not just look loud.
2. **One idea per image.** One headline, one visual, one promise. If the reader has to choose what to look at, they scroll. Every swipe in this doc passes this test. Cole Gordon's red card says one thing: "We pack your calendar with calls or you pay nothing."
3. **Readable at thumbnail size.** Test every image at 300px wide. If the headline is not readable there, it does not exist. Most winners use 3 to 8 word headlines set at roughly 8 to 12% of canvas height.
4. **Clear text hierarchy.** One dominant line (the hook), one supporting line (the payoff or detail), optionally one tiny line (source, date, disclaimer). Three sizes max. Brunson's FHL ads: giant "WHO WILL BE SPEAKING", medium "$24,535.97 IN FREE BONUSES", small "Watch the full reveal now."
5. **Faces and eyes.** A real face looking at the camera (or at the headline) is the most reliable attention hook. Founder photos beat stock. Dan Henry, Hormozi, Cardone, Gadzhi, Porterfield, Kutcher, Suby all lead with their own face. When the face looks toward the text, the eye follows.
6. **Native beats polished (for finding winners).** Notes-app screenshots, tweets, iPhone photos, plain text on a flat color. These do not trigger the "this is an ad" filter. Gadzhi's lo-fi photo with a cat and a Stories-style label runs right next to his polished version. Polished creative is for scaling a proven idea and for brand credibility, not for discovery.
7. **Proof on the image.** A number, a screenshot, a stack of books, a review count. Suby: a pallet of 10,000 books. Skool: "$2,000 MRR". Cole Gordon: "40 new sales calls last month." Proof that can be seen beats proof that is claimed.
8. **Offer clarity.** If there is an offer, the image should carry it: FREE, $997 crossed out, "5-Day Challenge", "$72 Free Welcome Kit". Motion's top format by hit rate is the "Offer-First Banner" (8.6% hit rate, 29.3% of spend).
9. **Small logo, or none.** Logo at 8 to 12% of canvas height, top-left or bottom-right. Centered or oversized logos signal "ad" before the reader decides to engage (Superscale). Founder-led ads often skip the logo entirely.

## 2. The proven static formats

Each format below has rules and the real swipes that show it. Formats are ranked roughly by how often they show up in long-running ads from the advertisers researched.

### (a) Big bold text-only / "ugly ad" / notes-app or tweet screenshot

The highest hit-rate asset type in Motion's 2026 data. No photo. Just words on a color.

**Rules:**
- One flat background color (red, black, navy, white, or a brand color). No gradients needed.
- One headline, 3 to 12 words, filling 60 to 80% of the width, centered.
- Font: heavy sans-serif (Inter Black, Archivo Black, Helvetica Bold) for the "shout" version. Serif or system font for the "note" version.
- Notes-app version: white or off-white card, black system text, short lines, one emoji max, phone-like padding.
- Tweet version: real tweet UI (avatar, name, handle, timestamp, like icons). The tweet is 1 to 2 sentences and ends with a pointer ("Click below to learn more 👇").
- Long-copy version works too: a paragraph of small text on a dark card. It reads as a letter, not an ad. Use only if the primary text is short, otherwise you repeat yourself.
- Never more than about 20% of canvas covered in ink for the shout version. The long-copy version breaks this rule on purpose, so treat it as its own test.

**Swipes:** `cole-gordon-red-text-only-pay-nothing.png`, `cole-gordon-tweet-screenshot-setter.png`, `cole-gordon-long-copy-40-calls.png`, `dan-martell-black-notes-text-apply.png`, `dan-martell-oct-26-27-big-type.png`, `hormozi-navy-text-roofing-owners.png`, `skool-black-card-side-hustle.png`, `grant-cardone-resignation-email-screenshot.png`

### (b) Founder or face + headline

The default for info products and coaching. Face on one side, headline on the other, or headline over the face.

**Rules:**
- Real photo of the founder, cut out or on a simple background. Eyes visible, looking at the camera or toward the headline.
- Headline is the hook from the primary text, shortened to 3 to 8 words, OR a "who this is for" callout ("Roofing owners doing $1M+ a year").
- The founder is doing something: holding a book, holding a sign, pointing, sitting at a table. A prop gives the eye a second thing to land on after the face.
- Speech-bubble variant: put the headline in a white rounded bubble beside the founder's head. Reads as the founder talking.
- Handwritten-sign variant: founder holds a real (or generated) whiteboard with the hook in marker. This is the highest-signal "ugly" version of this format.
- Run 3 to 6 audience variants with the same layout and only the callout changed (Hormozi: roofing / accounting / pest control / mortgage / law firms).

**Swipes:** `hormozi-holding-books-viral-ebook.png`, `hormozi-speech-bubble-real-estate.png`, `dan-henry-handwritten-sign-1m-webinar.png`, `dan-henry-change-my-mind-table.png`, `dan-henry-orange-knowing-how-to-close.png`, `iman-gadzhi-lofi-cat-last-chance.png`, `amy-porterfield-20k-to-100k-months.png`, `frank-kern-ojoy-free-accounts-blue.png`

### (c) Before / after or transformation

**Honest note:** none of the researched info-product or SaaS advertisers were running a literal split before/after static in the US in September 2026. The format is common in fitness, skin, home, and app verticals (Superscale reports a 2x CPI drop for one app using a before/after split). What the researched advertisers DO run is the transformation stated in words: a "from X to Y" headline, or a price before/after.

**Rules:**
- Literal version: two panels, left = before, right = after, same framing, same lighting, one small label each ("Before" / "After" or "Day 1" / "Day 30"). No headline needed beyond the labels.
- Word version: headline states the jump with numbers. "From $20,000 months to $100,000 months." "From zero subscribers to a real email list. In five days."
- Price version: old price struck through in red, new price large. "$1,000 → $497", "$97 → FREE".
- Never fake a physical before/after with AI for a real product claim. Meta will reject it and it is a policy violation. Use the word or price versions for offers.

**Swipes:** `amy-porterfield-20k-to-100k-months.png`, `grant-cardone-997-now-free.png`, `jenna-kutcher-free-mini-course-97-crossed.png`, `grant-cardone-3-day-summit-497.png`

### (d) Testimonial / review screenshot / proof stack

**Rules:**
- Quote in large serif or bold sans, in quotation marks, with the person's name and a one-line credential under it. Real names outperform initials.
- Put the quote on the photo of the person if you have it; otherwise on a flat color.
- Proof stack version: a number of things (10,000 books on pallets, 300+ creators, 6,500 reviews). Show the physical volume if it exists.
- "News" framing: a red "BREAKING NEWS" or "NEWS" tag above a headline written in third person ("Shark Tank investor gives away 10,000 books free"). Reads as coverage, not an ad.
- Stat card: one giant number and one small label ("$2,000 / MRR"). Nothing else.
- Screenshot of a real message (email, DM, text) works as proof AND as a pattern interrupt. Cardone runs a screenshot of a salesperson's resignation email as the image for an ad about compensation plans.

**Swipes:** `ag1-testimonial-quote-erica.png`, `sabri-suby-breaking-news-book-pallets.png`, `skool-2000-mrr-stat-card.png`, `cole-gordon-long-copy-40-calls.png`, `grant-cardone-resignation-email-screenshot.png`, `hubspot-loop-book-quote-card.png`

### (e) Product or book hero with benefit callouts

**Rules:**
- Product centered or slightly off-center, on a clean or in-context background, lit from one side.
- 2 to 4 callouts max, each a short label with a tiny icon or a thin line to the product. Callouts are 1 to 5 words.
- Headline above, one line. Optional small "Introducing" or "Free" tag.
- Book version: the book cover fills 40 to 60% of the frame, a big quote or the title's promise beside it. Or the founder holds the book (see format b).
- Flat-lay version for kits and bundles: every item laid out with its price or "Free" under it. Total value stated once.

### (f) Us vs Them comparison

**Honest note:** no researched advertiser had a classic two-column "Us vs Them" static live. The format remains standard in DTC (mattresses, supplements, software). Motion lists "Comparison" as a top static format for 2026. Rules are given so the skill can generate it.

**Rules:**
- Two columns. Left = "Them" (gray, plain, X marks). Right = "Us" (brand color, checkmarks). The Us column is slightly wider or brighter.
- 3 to 5 rows. Each row is 1 to 4 words. Rows must be true and provable.
- Headline above: "Why [product] instead of [category]?" or just the two names.
- Softer version: one column only, "The old way" struck through, "The new way" below it.
- Split-frame word version (seen at monday.com): left half "Work" with a messy visual, right half "...simplified." with a tidy one. Same idea, no table.

**Swipes:** none live from the researched set. See `monday-ui-screenshot-gazillion.png` for the "old way / new way" energy in a single frame.

### (g) Meme / native / lo-fi

**Rules:**
- Shot on a phone. Slight grain, uneven light, real room. Nothing cropped to perfection.
- Text is a Stories-style label (white pill with black text, or black bar with white text), not a designed headline.
- Or borrow a meme template the audience already knows ("Change My Mind" table sign). Put the claim on the sign.
- Or make it look like the product's own UI: a quiz, a poll, a "select the correct image" screen. Duolingo's static ads are just screens from the app with a question on top.
- Include a small imperfection on purpose (a cat, a can of drink, a plant). It sells "real."
- Same headline as the polished version. Test them head to head.

**Swipes:** `iman-gadzhi-lofi-cat-last-chance.png`, `dan-henry-change-my-mind-table.png`, `duolingo-quiz-time-native.png`, `ag1-ugc-scoop-iphone-shot.png`, `cole-gordon-tweet-screenshot-setter.png`

### (h) Offer / discount / free-thing callout

The "Offer-First Banner" is Motion's top format by hit rate (8.6%) and spend share (29.3%).

**Rules:**
- The offer is the headline. "FREE", "$997 NOW FREE", "Get Your $72 Free Welcome Kit", "We're giving away free accounts this week."
- Strike-through the old price in red or gray. New price or "FREE" in the loudest color on the canvas (yellow, green, or white on red).
- Add one urgency badge if true: "Will sell out", "This week", "Register before the deadline."
- Event version: date + city + one benefit line + a button-shaped CTA on the image.
- Color variants: Frank Kern runs the same "Free oJoy.ai accounts this week" layout in blue, red, pink, and yellow. Same words, different canvas. Cheap way to get 4 to 6 tests.

**Swipes:** `grant-cardone-997-now-free.png`, `grant-cardone-3-day-summit-497.png`, `frank-kern-ojoy-free-accounts-blue.png`, `russell-brunson-fhl-who-is-speaking-bonuses.png`, `ag1-72-free-welcome-kit-flatlay.png`, `clickfunnels-first-1000-free-5-day.png`

### (i) "Free training / workshop" lead-magnet cards

**Rules:**
- Word "FREE" appears once, large, or as a badge (Kutcher's "$97 → FREE" orange tab).
- Headline is the outcome, 4 to 9 words: "How to Grow an Email List of Buyers", "From zero subscribers to a real email list. In five days."
- Show the deliverable: a phone or tablet mockup of the guide, a workbook cover, a mini-course card.
- One button-shaped CTA on the image: "Sign up now", "Grab the free mini-course", "Get my free ticket."
- Founder avatar in a circle at the top is optional and adds trust.
- Pastel or bright single-color backgrounds work well here because the format is expected to look friendly, not corporate.

**Swipes:** `jenna-kutcher-free-class-email-list.png`, `jenna-kutcher-free-mini-course-97-crossed.png`, `jenna-kutcher-pinterest-guide-mockup.png`, `clickfunnels-first-1000-free-5-day.png`, `iman-gadzhi-lofi-cat-last-chance.png`, `dan-henry-orange-knowing-how-to-close.png`

### (j) Chart / dashboard / results screenshot

**Rules:**
- A real (or realistic) screenshot of the product UI, a dashboard, a Stripe or Skool earnings panel. Slight tilt or a soft drop shadow so it reads as a screenshot, not a flat design.
- One headline over it. The screenshot is proof of the headline, not decoration.
- Highlight one number. Circle it, or make it the only large text.
- Keep the UI legible at thumbnail size: crop tight to the one panel that matters.
- Stat-card version: one big number, one label, nothing else (Skool "$2,000 MRR").

**Swipes:** `monday-ui-screenshot-gazillion.png`, `skool-2000-mrr-stat-card.png`, `grant-cardone-resignation-email-screenshot.png`

### (k) Quote card

**Rules:**
- One quote, 6 to 20 words, in a serif (editorial feel) or heavy sans (punchy feel).
- Author line under it, small. Or a small "Keep reading →" if the quote is the hook and the ad copy continues it.
- Ground is a flat color, off-white, or a black-and-white photo of the speaker with the quote over the negative space.
- Yellow highlight on the key phrase is a proven trick (MasterClass: "YOU NEED TO STAND OUT" in yellow inside a white quote).
- The quote should be a belief, a contradiction, or a rule. Not a feature.

## 3. Copy ON the image

### Headline formulas that work on images (3 to 8 words)

- **Who it's for:** "[Audience] doing [milestone], this is for you." (Hormozi)
- **Guarantee:** "[Result] or you pay nothing." (Cole Gordon)
- **Big number:** "$997 → FREE". "10,000 books free." "$2,000 MRR."
- **Contradiction:** "The best webinars teach almost nothing." (Dan Henry)
- **Change my mind:** "[Bold claim]. Change my mind."
- **Last chance:** "LAST CHANCE to [outcome] with me live."
- **Question:** "Who will be speaking at [event]?!?"
- **Free thing:** "We're giving away free [thing] this week."
- **Outcome in a timeframe:** "From zero subscribers to a real list. In five days."
- **News:** "[Credible person] gives away [thing] just to prove it works."
- **Quote:** "Being a nice person who's good at your job is nowhere near enough."

Rules: no more than 8 words in the dominant line. Numbers as digits. One capital-letter style per image (either ALL CAPS shout or sentence case, not both in the same line).

### Font sizing for mobile

Working on a 1080px-wide canvas:
- Dominant headline: 90 to 140px (about 8 to 12% of a 1350px-tall canvas). Heavier weight is better than bigger size.
- Supporting line: 44 to 64px.
- Tiny line (source, date, disclaimer): 28 to 36px minimum. Anything smaller vanishes in feed.
- Line length: 3 to 5 words per line for the headline. Break lines by meaning, not by width.
- Check: shrink the image to 300px wide. The dominant line must still be readable.

### Where to place text (safe zones)

- **1:1 (1080x1080):** keep text inside the center 80%. 108px margin on all sides.
- **4:5 (1080x1350):** keep text inside the center; leave about 14% clear at top (~190px) and about 20% clear at bottom (~270px). The feed UI (page name, headline, CTA button) sits under the image, so bottom-heavy text competes with it.
- **9:16 (1080x1920):** keep everything inside the middle 1080x1420. Top 250px clear (profile bar), bottom 340px clear (CTA, caption, reply bar). Sides 6% clear (about 65px).
- Put the headline in the top third or dead center. Bottom third is for a CTA or nothing.

### How image text relates to primary text and link headline

Do not repeat. Escalate.
- **Image headline** = the hook, or the offer, in 3 to 8 words. It earns the read.
- **Primary text** (above the image) = the argument. It can be long. It opens with the same idea in a fuller sentence, then proves it.
- **Link headline** (under the image) = the action or the deliverable. "Download Free Scaling Frameworks." "Get My Free Ticket." "Reserve Your Ticket."
- Test: read image → link headline → primary text. If any two say the same thing in the same words, change one.
- Hormozi example: image says "Roofing owners doing $1M+ a year, these 5 Scaling Frameworks are for you." Primary text opens "Business Owners, real quick, can I get your email? I want to send you the exact frameworks we've used to scale." Link headline: "Download Free Scaling Frameworks." Three layers, no repeats.

### CTA on the image: yes or no?

- **Yes** for offers, events, lead magnets, and anything with a deadline. Draw it as a button (rounded pill, solid fill, 2 to 4 words: "Learn More", "Get My Free Ticket", "Sign Up Now"). Place in the bottom third, inside the safe zone.
- **No** for ugly ads, tweets, notes, quote cards, UGC photos, and news-style ads. A button breaks the disguise. Let the link headline do the CTA.
- Never two CTAs on one image.

## 4. Visual direction

**Color and contrast**
- Pick ONE dominant field color and ONE accent. Red/white, navy/white, black/white, yellow/black, brand blue/white, off-white/dark text.
- Text contrast ratio at least 7:1 (white on red, white on navy, black on yellow, black on off-white). Never light gray on white or mid-blue on mid-purple.
- Yellow highlight bar behind a key phrase is the cheapest way to add hierarchy.
- Red is the most common urgency accent across every advertiser researched. Green is used for "FREE" and money.

**Backgrounds**
- Solid flat color: best for text-only, offers, quotes, stat cards.
- Subtle gradient (one hue, dark to darker): fine for event and founder cutouts. Avoid rainbow or multi-hue gradients; they read as template.
- Real photo: for founder, UGC, product-in-use. Darken 20 to 40% behind text or place text on the empty side.
- Blurred screenshot or UI: for SaaS; the product is the background and the headline floats over it.
- Off-white paper texture: for notes, letters, and editorial quote cards.

**Photography style**
- Candid iPhone: front-camera or friend-shot, natural light, slight grain, real room. Use for discovery and lo-fi tests.
- Studio cutout: founder on a flat color or gradient, soft key light, slight rim light. Use for scaling and for offers.
- Event photos: stage, crowd, energy. Use for summits and live events.
- Product: one light source, soft shadow, in a real context (kitchen counter, desk) rather than pure white when possible.

**Composition**
- One focal point. Face, product, or headline. Never three.
- Leave negative space where the text goes. Plan the empty side before you shoot or prompt.
- Rule of thirds for face + text: face on one third line, text on the other two thirds.
- Subject should touch or point at the headline when possible (Cardone's speaker on stage under the summit title; Henry's mug and sign).

**Brand consistency**
- Same 1 to 2 fonts across every ad. Same accent color. Same logo position (small, corner) or no logo.
- Vary the format, not the brand system. Kern's four color variants keep type, layout, and photo identical.
- A one-ground, one-accent system (a dark neutral ground with a single saturated accent field, one display font plus one mono or grotesque) fits formats (a), (h), (i), (j), and (k) directly. Use the brand colors and fonts from the user's profile as that system.

## 5. Prompting guide: gpt-image-2 vs Nano Banana Pro / Nano Banana 2

> **Skill rule:** the /ad-image-gen skill uses only **gpt-image-2** (default) and **Nano Banana Pro**. Nano Banana 2 is documented below for comparison only. It is not offered as an option, because it loses on text accuracy and realism, and the cost saving doesn't matter at ad volumes.

### Which model for what (2026, from published tests)

| Job | Pick | Why |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Accurate text on the image | **gpt-image-2** first | OpenAI's model is built for text-heavy images; 2026 head-to-heads put it at roughly 99% character accuracy vs 94% for Nano Banana Pro (nanobanana.org), and it renders small body text as real words where NBP produced pseudo-text (OakGen). Nano Banana 2 is close on short headlines; NBP is fine for 1 to 3 short text elements. |
| (b) Face / likeness from a reference photo | **gpt-image-2** for one person, strict likeness | OpenAI documents gpt-image-2 as the pick for "identity-sensitive edits" and multi-image compositing; input fidelity is high by default. XDA's test found gpt-image-2 kept face, hair, eyes, and head position while Nano Banana's output drifted. Nano Banana 2 accepts more references (up to 4 characters, 10 objects, 3 style refs; Pro: 5 characters, 6 objects) and is better when you need several people or products in one scene. |
| (c) Photorealism (skin, lighting, product shots) | **Nano Banana Pro** | Scored 9.4 to 9.5 vs 7.5 to 7.8 on portrait realism in OakGen's 20-prompt test; strongest at lighting and skin. Use it for founder cutouts, UGC-style photos, product heroes. |
| (d) Editing an existing image | **Either. Nano Banana Pro slightly cleaner** | NBP edits drifted less across chained edits (8.8 vs 8.5, OakGen). gpt-image-2 is stronger when the edit involves changing or adding text. Both accept natural-language edits; Gemini supports semantic masking. |
| (e) Aspect ratios | **Both cover Meta's three sizes** | Nano Banana 2/Pro: preset ratios including 1:1, 4:5, 9:16, 16:9, 3:4, 2:3, 21:9, and resolution tiers 1K / 2K / 4K. gpt-image-2: any custom size up to 3840px on the long edge, both edges multiples of 16, max 3:1 ratio; common sizes 1024x1024, 1024x1536, 2560x1440. Note gpt-image-2 has no native 4:5 preset; request 1088x1360 (multiples of 16) or generate 1024x1280. |

Other facts worth knowing:
- Model IDs: `gpt-image-2` (OpenAI Images API, `images.generate` and `images.edit`), `gemini-3-pro-image` (Nano Banana Pro), `gemini-3.1-flash-image` (Nano Banana 2), `gemini-3.1-flash-lite-image` (Nano Banana 2 Lite).
- Every Gemini image carries a SynthID watermark (invisible). gpt-image-2 images carry C2PA metadata. Neither affects Meta delivery.
- Pricing (fal.ai, 2026): gpt-image-2 high quality about $0.21 per 1024x1024; Nano Banana 2 about $0.08 at 1K and $0.16 at 4K; Nano Banana Pro about $0.13.
- gpt-image-2 supports `quality: low | medium | high`. Use `high` for any image with text. Use `low` for layout ideation.
- Both models read plain English. Keyword spam ("8k, masterpiece, trending") does nothing.

### Universal rules for ad prompts

1. Put the exact headline in quotes, and say "verbatim, exactly as written, no other text." Spell tricky words letter by letter if needed.
2. Name the font style, weight, color, case, and position. "Heavy sans-serif, all caps, white, centered in the top third."
3. Name the aspect ratio and the safe zones. "4:5 vertical. Keep all text inside the middle 70% vertically."
4. Say "no extra text, no watermark, no logo unless specified, no fake UI elements."
5. Say what stays constant when editing. "Keep the person's face, hair, expression, and pose exactly the same. Change only the background."
6. For a reference face: attach the photo, then write "Use the attached photo as the exact likeness of the person. Same face, skin tone, hairline, facial hair, and eyes. Do not beautify or age."
7. One change per follow-up prompt. Do not rewrite the whole thing to fix one word.
8. Generate at 2K or 1080-plus width. Meta downsizes; you do not need 4K.
9. Ask for the text to be "cleanly rendered, high contrast, legible at small sizes."
10. Write the prompt in this order: canvas and ratio → background → subject → text (exact) → typography → constraints.

### QA checklist for every generated image

- [ ] Every word on the image matches the brief exactly. Read it letter by letter. Check numbers, currency symbols, apostrophes, and brand capitalization (oJoy.ai, monday.com).
- [ ] No extra text, phantom words, gibberish in the background, or fake UI the brief did not ask for.
- [ ] Face: matches the reference. Eyes symmetrical, teeth normal, hands have five fingers, glasses frames continuous, no warped ears or jewelry.
- [ ] Shrink to 300px wide. Dominant headline readable? Face recognizable?
- [ ] Text sits inside the safe zone for the target ratio (Section 6).
- [ ] Contrast: white on dark or black on light. No mid-tone text on mid-tone ground.
- [ ] One focal point. Nothing competing with the headline.
- [ ] Logo small or absent. No watermark.
- [ ] Ratio and pixel size correct (1080x1080, 1080x1350, or 1080x1920). Upscale or re-generate if smaller.
- [ ] Nothing that violates Meta policy: no fake before/after body claims, no "you" callouts about personal attributes, no fake news branding of real outlets, no implied celebrity endorsement you do not have.
- [ ] Export as PNG for text-heavy images (sharper text), JPG for photos. Under 30MB (easy).

## 6. Sizes and specs for Meta placements (2026)

| Placement | Ratio | Pixels | Text safe zone | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Feed (FB + IG), default | **4:5** | **1080 x 1350** | About 14% clear at top, 20% clear at bottom; keep text inside center 80% width | Takes about a third more vertical screen than 1:1 and consistently lifts CTR. Use this as the default. |
| Feed square | 1:1 | 1080 x 1080 | 108px margin all sides | Works everywhere, including right column and Marketplace. Second file to build. |
| Stories / Reels | 9:16 | 1080 x 1920 | Middle 1080 x 1420: top 250px clear, bottom 340px clear, 6% (about 65px) clear on each side | Profile bar on top, CTA and reply bar at bottom cover anything outside. |
| Right column / landscape | 1.91:1 | 1200 x 628 | Center 80% | Low priority. Only if you want desktop right-column. |

- File: JPG or PNG, up to 30 MB. Most ads are 200KB to 2MB.
- Text limits around the image: primary text truncates around 125 characters ("See more" after), headline 40 characters (27 is safest), description 30 characters.
- There is no longer a 20% text rule enforced by Meta. Text-heavy images deliver fine. The 20% figure now shows up only as a performance finding (Superscale), not a policy.
- Recommended default: **build 4:5 first, then 9:16, then 1:1**. Use placement customization to assign 9:16 to Stories/Reels. If you only build one, build 4:5; Meta crops it to 1:1 where needed (design so the center square still works).

## 7. Testing framework

**How many variants**
- Benchmark: one new ad per $3,000 of monthly spend, with 60 to 70% of them static (Taylor Sicard). $10K/month = 3 to 4 new ads a week. $30K/month = about 10 a week.
- A minimum test batch is 4 to 6 statics on one offer. Fewer than 4 and you cannot see a pattern.
- Expect 1 in 20 to win. Do not judge the batch by the average; judge it by the best one.

**What to vary first (in this order)**
1. **Hook / headline on the image.** Highest variance. Same layout, 4 to 6 different headlines (Hormozi's audience callouts, Henry's three sign lines).
2. **Format.** Take the winning headline and rebuild it as text-only, founder + headline, tweet, and offer card. Motion's data: the best accounts test text-forward and UGC-style first, then move winners into polished versions.
3. **Background / color.** Same words, same layout, 3 to 4 canvas colors (Kern's blue / red / pink / yellow). Cheapest test, smallest lift, run it last.
4. Only then touch the primary text or link headline. Change one thing at a time.

**How to read results**
- Give each variant enough data before judging: 5 to 10 conversions, or spend equal to 5 to 10x your target CPA (Taylor Sicard). Superscale's stricter bar for calling something a "proven" static is 50 conversions and CPA within 15% when budget goes from $1K to $10K.
- Winner = lowest CPA (or cost per lead) with CTR above 1% and holds for at least 7 days.
- Kill signals: CTR under 1%, CTR down 20%+ week over week, frequency above 3.0 on prospecting, CPA rising with nothing else changed.
- Statics fatigue in 7 to 14 days at scale. Have the next batch ready before the current one dies. Refresh a winner by changing color or headline before rebuilding from scratch.
- Log every test: hypothesis, variable changed, winner, margin, date. Feed winners back into `writing-ad-copy-101.md`'s swipe file and into this doc's Section 8.
